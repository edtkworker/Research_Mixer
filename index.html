The A-squared-E Lab launched in October 2025. Our vision is for Wichita State to become a regional leader and a nationally recognized center of excellence in applied research on GenAI in education.

Our mission has three parts: applied research that turns into practical classroom guidance; collaboration across WSU, local school districts, and outside partners; and professional development that helps teachers integrate AI responsibly.

About 50 seconds, one click.

Everything we do comes back to one overarching question: how can GenAI be integrated into education in ways that strengthen, rather than replace, human learning and judgment?

[Click] We work on it from four directions. First, we study how educators and learners are actually using GenAI. Second, we build theory, such as Generativism, the framework Dr. Mara Alagic and I published this year. Third, we develop tools that support learning without taking over the learner’s judgment; our undergraduate fellows are building two of them. And fourth, we develop classroom strategies that foster epistemic responsibility, meaning staying accountable for what you accept as true.

About 70 seconds.

Two projects are active right now. The first is GenAI in the K-12 Classroom, a mixed-methods study of how teachers are adopting GenAI and what support they need. It is funded by WSU’s URCA program, and we are collecting data now.

The second is an autoethnography. I deliberately learned two financial topics I knew little about, retirement accounts and currency hedging, through conversations with GenAI, and then analyzed my own dialogues. What is emerging is a provisional four-phase model: grounding, contestation, reconstruction, and synthesis. That manuscript is in preparation.

Our two undergraduate fellows also lead their own projects: Evander Davis on AI-assisted STEM tutoring, and Urshitha Chakka on AI learning assistants grounded in course materials. They present their work separately.
